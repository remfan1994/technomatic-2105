TECHNOMATIC 2105 v30: independent native review

Scope

Read-only review compared MusicEngine.cpp/.h with the supplied v29
baseline. The review focused on fixed instrument identity, generated
recipe reconstruction, final note bounds, event scheduling, foreground
handoffs and real-time resource use. The reviewer did not edit those
production files; concrete findings were sent to the engine owner for
scoped fixes. Intentional retirement of channel controls is not treated
as a regression.

Concrete findings and final disposition

1. Foreground harmony could be discarded behind an existing pad.

The first v30 schedulePad guard returned whenever a sustained pad was
present, including when the new event was the foreground answer or final
cadence. Since phrase planning can hand the melody to harmony and mute
other accompaniment, that guard could drop the intended answer.

Foreground scheduling now cancels pending accompaniment pad events and
releases the active pad over eight milliseconds before starting the new
foreground pad. The cadence uses the same priority behavior. A focused
48 kHz render probe observed the new voice starting at sample 385,
nonzero new-voice output and at most one active pad during that handoff.
The persistent regression in tools/validate_v30.cpp also checks pending
event replacement, actual voice activation, finite output and the
one-active-pad foreground transition. That final validator passes.

This guarantee applies to foreground replacement. Ordinary accompaniment
can intentionally overlap the final tail of a pad with less than 120 ms
remaining. A universal claim that there is never any pad overlap would
be incorrect.

2. Edited snapshot values were remapped on every load.

Both the v29 baseline and initial v30 source confused raw edit inputs
with rounded effective display summaries. The original two cases showed
different PCM after the very first snapshot reload. The final v30 raw
editrecipe/edit_* representation fixes this without treating every
display field as an explicit edit. All 40 control fields, absent-field
semantics and three successive reloads are covered by the public-API
regression in tools/edited_snapshot_v30.cpp. Its final result is 48 cases,
144 reloads and 23,040,000 exactly equal float samples; see
EDITED_SNAPSHOT_V30_VALIDATION.txt for inputs, counts and limits.

3. A muted primary lead could silence an enabled foreground owner.

The initial v30 answer scheduler multiplied a promoted bass or support
voice by the primary lead weight. With an edited lead=0/bass=100 recipe,
the planner could suppress normal bass accompaniment for a bass-owned
answer and then schedule no foreground notes. Phrase gain now follows
the actual owner. Muted foundation instruments also remain muted through
arrivals, cadence and silence recovery. The final native validator
checks promoted bass with a muted lead, disabled bass/harmony and a
fully muted finite export. These checks pass.

Additional reviewed areas

Final note-bound repair runs after device, harmony-owner and cadence
transformations. The separate validator stresses edited root extremes,
private octave variation, final slow foreground notes and triggered
harmony components. This closes the bounds issue independently raised
by the main reviewer before this review began.

The generated catalogue indices and per-support model lookups are
bounded. Models remain fixed for a composition; changing tone does not
select a different model. The new catalogue does not create extra voice
roles merely because more instrument designs are available. The final
paired palette and scheduled-event audits provide the actual coverage.

The new phrase-planning and foreground-note work uses fixed-size local
arrays. No new per-sample heap allocation was found. The fixed event and
voice pools remain bounded; no concrete new queue-index or lifetime
defect was identified in the reviewed paths. These are code-review
observations, not a proof that every possible composition meets a device
deadline. The inherited candidate search and snapshot/history work on a
new-piece transition still need real Android timing coverage.
