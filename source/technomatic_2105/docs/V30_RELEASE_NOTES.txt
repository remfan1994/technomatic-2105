TECHNOMATIC 2105 v30
Version 0.30.0 / Android versionCode 42
2026-10-18 UTC

PURPOSE

v30 expands the available musical vocabulary while preserving the size of
each song's ensemble. Its center remains a recognizable generated theme,
complementary answers and continuing improvisation. There is one coherent
generator, without separate radio-accessibility and demonstration modes.

The owner's Bismillah intention and the importance of reciprocal musical
relationships are recorded in the project guidance. The musical implementation
uses thematic relationships, space, developing phrases and exchanges between
existing instruments. It does not add sound effects or commercial references.

MORE INSTRUMENT CHOICES, SAME ENSEMBLE RULES

                         v29       v30
  Bass models             12        20
  Melodic models          16        32
  Harmony/pad models      10        18
  Drum-kit families        8        12

New designs include odd-harmonic reed-like bodies, rounded struck tones,
controlled saturated voices and sustained tones with gradual harmonic opening.
They are equations and generated parameters, not recorded instruments or
commercial patches. Attack, release, glide, articulation and harmonic balance
vary within those families.

The v29 rule selecting supporting roles is retained. Lead, bass and harmony
each keep one synthesis family. Supporting melodic roles share at most three
additional families, for at most six pitched synthesis families in a song.
The fixed voice/event capacities are unchanged. Only the selected model is
evaluated for a sounding voice; the whole catalogue is not rendered at once.

In a paired audit of 256 seeds, every seed kept its v29 supporting-role count.
Across 128 phrases per seed, the mean number of actually triggered pitched
families fell from 5.996 to 4.836, and the maximum fell from nine to six. This
reduction removes incidental family switches caused by tone offsets. These
counts describe instrument families across a song, not simultaneous notes.

CHANNELS RETIRED

Channels are removed from the main interface, history and Metadata Editor.
Old channel preferences are discarded without clearing unrelated user fields.
Legacy native setters are inert. Loading an older recipe cannot restore a
named-channel profile. The full generator supplies the music.

An internal synthesis design named VelvetDrift remains distinct from the
removed Velvet Circuit channel; it is not an active channel selector.

WHAT THE REPETITION INVESTIGATION FOUND

The user did not supply a reproducible seed/time range for the rare repetitive
example, so the investigation used a deterministic sample of 256 seeds and
512 post-introduction bars per seed.

The old pieces were not literally almost all chorus: Hook sections occupied
about 21.9 percent of the measured bars on average. The stronger cause was
foreground reuse. The primary line used only 4-9 distinct note/rest rhythm
patterns per seed, with a median of seven. Changing accompaniment or tone
could make the total arrangement appear more varied while that line repeated.

The new phrase plan coordinates 10-18-bar development cycles. One or two bars
recall the theme; related variations and exchanges develop it, followed by a
question, arrival and release. Interior note placement and contour can change
while retaining phrase anchors and relationships to the generated theme.

Final paired measurements:

  Median distinct primary rhythm plans:           7 -> 167
  Median distinct foreground note/rest plans:   147 -> 459.5 of 512 bars
  Worst top-four foreground phrase occupancy:   187 -> 95 of 512 bars
  Longest identical primary-turn run:              4 -> 2

The v30 main lead also develops independently of instrument handoffs: its
median is 314 distinct plans across a median 363 main-lead turns. Instrument
identity, gain changes and different timbres are not counted as new melodies.
The distinct-plan metrics count exact chord-relative note/rest plans. They
are measures of structural development, not a score for perceived quality.

Two measured weak cases:

  Seed 3041712678, 61.72 BPM:
    v29: 87 distinct foreground plans across 512 bars.
    v30: 471 across 512; main lead has 318 across its 352 turns.

  Seed 2514750925, 86.39 BPM:
    v29: 99 distinct foreground plans across 512 bars.
    v30: 477 across 512; main lead has 322 across its 351 turns.

Short same-seed audio comparisons are supplied separately. Because v30 changes
synthesis and composition logic together, those comparisons are whole-engine
comparisons, not an isolated experiment on one parameter.

FIVE COORDINATED MUSICAL DEVICES

Counterweight
  An answering voice changes the latter phrase's direction and span around a
  generated exchange point, keeping a relationship with the theme.

Pocket Exchange
  Interior notes move within bounded rhythmic windows while preserving their
  order and important phrase anchors. An existing voice can take the lead.

Held Question
  The line leaves a continuing or suspended thought while drums and harmony
  make room for the ending of the phrase.

Confluence
  Melody reaches a chord member and bass reaches the root at a shared generated
  arrival point, with coordinated emphasis and a requested harmony arrival.

Open Release
  The next phrase reduces activity and leaves space after the arrival. Some
  releases let the existing harmony instrument carry the foreground.

Answers promote an instrument already selected for the song. Its accompaniment
part yields rather than adding another complete melody. A sustained old pad
now releases before a new foreground harmony answer; the tested replacement
emits after 385 samples at 48 kHz with no overlapping active pad. Ordinary
accompaniment can still have brief release-tail overlaps.

SLOW MUSIC

The older weak tail included very high lead registers, repeated root endings
and articulation that did not always follow the developed phrase. For example,
seed 3816266512 averaged MIDI 92.75 in the measured old lead plan, with 505 of
512 phrases ending on the root. v30 averages MIDI 67.11 for the same seed's
main lead and gives its foreground phrases 92 root endings out of 512.

The fixes use MIDI 52-78 for slow non-bass foreground and MIDI 45-76 for actual
slow harmony components, including generated arrivals and closing notes.
Octave choices preserve pitch classes and prefer nearby notes. Harmony can
span two or three bars. Interior phrases may finish on another chord member
or a suspension; recalls and arrivals keep their resolving role. Note duration
follows the actual gaps in the developed slow phrase.

Across the measured slow subset, root-ending foreground phrases fell from
80.3 percent to 20.7 percent. This is a consequence of the phrase roles, not
a target imposed on every song. All four slow-to-intense musical intents remain.

ORIGINALITY AND THE REPORTED GLITTERING CUE

No imported commercial musical content was identified in the inspected v28,
v29 or v30 project. Their music paths use local synthesis and generated grammar.
The source inventories and both final APKs contain no audio assets, soundfonts
or MIDI songs, and have no INTERNET permission. Existing Oboe audio-I/O code
is attributed in NOTICE; general scales and synthesis techniques are building
blocks, not a claim that all underlying mathematics was newly invented.

The removed Velvet Circuit profile cannot reactivate. Regular high sparkle
and bell placements, upper-register afterimages and recurring rising chord
cascades were replaced with generated phrase-related instrument responses.
Several fractional partial relationships on wrapped oscillator phases were
revised to harmonic ratios, avoiding the associated repeated discontinuities.

These address identifiable causes of chiming/reward-like behavior. There was
no recording or seed for the reported Zelda resemblance, so its precise cause
could not be confirmed. Source inspection cannot guarantee that no generated
short phrase will ever resemble existing music. No commercial cue was fetched,
sampled or transcribed for the new designs.

METADATA AND EXACT REPLAY

The Metadata Editor automatically shows the current identity:

  Seed: 141414419
  Engine: 30

User notes remain editable separately. Both OGG OpusTags and FLAC comments
physically embed seed, engine, export range, user notes and the complete recipe.
Legacy channel tags are explicitly neutral, not active sound selections.
Metadata is captured once for the export; later playback or editor changes
cannot retag an export already running.

The full recipe matters: it retains the winning candidate and any exact edit
inputs as well as the seed. An inherited edited-recipe bug reapplied rounded
derived summaries as fresh controls, causing drift on reload. Canonical v30
snapshots instead retain the exact original edit inputs and their presence.

All 48 edited-recipe regression cases passed three successive reloads, covering
all 40 supported controls: 144 reloads, 12,582 assertions and 23,040,000 exact
float samples compared. Both original drift cases replayed 12 seconds per pass
with zero differing samples. A muted lead no longer silences a bass-owned
answer, and disabled foundation roles remain muted through arrivals and export.

Metadata verification passed 1,049 Java checks. MediaInfo, ffprobe, opusinfo and
metaflac exposed the expected physical fields. FLAC recovered all 1,536,000
source PCM bytes exactly; nine OGG tag-rewrite cases retained identical decoded
audio and timing. The production playback controls passed 93 host checks.

Useful inspection commands after copying a recording from the phone:

  opusinfo track.ogg
  mediainfo track.ogg
  ffprobe -v error -show_entries stream_tags:format_tags -of json track.ogg
  metaflac --export-tags-to=- track.flac
  mediainfo track.flac

BUILD, VALIDATION AND INSTALLATION

Final assembleDebug, assembleRelease and lintDebug succeeded using the requested
NDK. All three native ABIs built: arm64-v8a, armeabi-v7a and x86_64. Lint reports
0 errors and 20 warnings. Both APKs passed ZIP CRC and manifest/version checks;
the debug signature verified. The native suite passed 63 checks spanning
catalogue coverage, foreground development and ownership, register limits,
palette bounds, sample-exact snapshots, finite exports and continuing playback.

The full 63-check native suite also passed AddressSanitizer and
UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer with no reported errors. Leak detection was disabled
for a host restriction; this run is not a leak-freedom certification.

The debug APK uses vip.thatiam.technomatic2105.debug and can coexist with a
release installation. Its certificate differs from the supplied v29 debug
APK, so it cannot directly update that APK. Build the source locally with the
existing installation's signing key for an in-place update. The release APK
is intentionally unsigned and requires the owner's release key. No private
signing key is included.

No physical Android phone or emulator was used. The included listening examples
are host renders. Device playback speed, MediaCodec/MediaStore behavior and
keyboard/rotation remain device checks. Structural tests do not certify musical
taste, and a finite deterministic generator cannot guarantee that every possible
note sequence is unique over mathematical infinity. Motif recurrence is intended;
ordinary playback continues developing the current seed without a fixed audio
loop or automatic replacement.

v30 interprets older seeds and recipes with its new engine and ignores retired
channels. It does not recreate v29 waveforms exactly. Preserve old recordings
and the old project for exact historical playback.
