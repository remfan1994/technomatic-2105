PURPOSE

This revision addresses the two volume problems reported after v30:

1. The output mixer held average level low and limited even strong sample
   peaks to about 64 percent of digital full scale.
2. The mixer and composition also raised and lowered volume over time,
   including coordinated part-volume swells and dips for musical effect.

Both are corrected in live playback and offline exports. The v30 music
generator remains: its expanded original instrument catalogue, same ensemble
size, slow-music grammar, recognizable themes, complementary phrases and
continuing improvisation. Channels remain discontinued. This patch adds no
audio recordings, samples, background noise or special effects.

FIX 1: FULL-RANGE OUTPUT

Removed the attenuating master setting, RMS automatic gain control and old
final limiting/saturation chain responsible for the roughly 64-percent bound.
App output gain is fixed at unity. Android's system media volume stays under
the listener's control; the app does not change that setting.

The output sample range is now [-1.0, 1.0], up to 100 percent of digital full
scale. There is no replacement 94-percent or 98-percent ceiling. A fixed,
memoryless peak curve rounds unusually large peaks, with a knee at 0.80 and
a ceiling of 1.0. The knee is where rounding begins, not the maximum output.
Below that knee the new stage is linear. It has no level detector, attack or
release controller, gain-recovery envelope, lookahead, normalization, added
latency or persistent gain state. Channels are processed independently.

The original fixed per-song tonal drive and DC filter remain. Their purpose
is tone/filtering; they do not ride the volume during a song. The full curve
and retained processing are documented in docs/OUTPUT_V30_1_DESIGN.txt.

FIX 2: NO VOLUME AUTOMATION FOR MUSICAL EFFECT

Removed kick-triggered whole-mix ducking, automatic gain riding, transition
fades, the legacy fade/dead-air switch state and the final export tail fade.
Both Next paths now change composition immediately.

Also removed continuous phrase and section multipliers from all nine note
amplitude sites: kick, snare/clap, closed hats, open hats, percussion, bass,
harmony, foreground and supporting voices. Intro/outro volume ramps,
Hook/Surge boosts, Shadow/OpenRelease dips, Confluence gain accents and
long-term section level drift no longer turn those parts up and down.

The existing phrase factors still determine note activity, probabilities
and structural entrances/exits. Previously silent structural events remain
silent. Fixed role balance, existing user/support weights, individual note
accents, rolls, flams, rests and instrument attack/decay/release remain.
Notes can start, end and leave space without a moving master volume or
coordinated positive-amplitude envelope.

Fixed gain does not make every waveform sample or musical passage equally
loud. A rest is still a rest, and fewer simultaneous notes produce less
sound. This revision removes the controls that intentionally swell or dip
the band's level; it does not flatten the music into a constant waveform.

MEASURED RESULT

Sixteen declared seeds were rendered before and after the patch at 48 kHz
stereo for 88 seconds each. The set covers slow, groove, driving and intense
intents, including sparse candidates selected before loudness measurement.
No mastering normalization was used. The settled measurement window is
[40,88) seconds; startup [0,10) and full [0,88) windows were also measured.

Settled window:
  Original v30 median loudness:             -19.65 LUFS
  v30.1 median loudness:                    -14.05 LUFS
  Median paired loudness improvement:       +5.85 LU
  Individual paired improvements:           +4.0 to +7.2 LU
  Median paired RMS improvement:            +6.08 dB

The difference of the two medians is not the median of paired differences;
both statistics are reported deliberately. Full-window paired improvement
was +5.95 LU at the median; startup improvement was +6.0 LU at the median.

The largest native sample measured was -0.0139845 dBFS, about 99.84 percent
of full scale. No nonfinite or out-of-range source samples occurred in the
matrix. The added peak curve operated on a median 0.0271 percent of samples
per tune; its maximum fraction was 0.9235 percent in the sampled set.

The four included 48-second A/B pairs use these identical seed excerpts:

  Intent / seed                 v30 LUFS    v30.1 LUFS    Change
  Slow / 1013904226                 -20.1         -14.4      +5.7
  Groove / 2654435761               -20.5         -16.0      +4.5
  Driving / 3711502885              -21.2         -16.4      +4.8
  Intense / 3668339987               -17.3         -10.1      +7.2

The driving example is the quietest settled seed in the declared set. These
are examples from a limited test matrix, not guarantees for every seed or
every phone. The measured gain addresses sustained level as well as peaks.

FULL-SCALE AND CODEC MEASUREMENT LIMITS

The sample bound is not a universal reconstructed true-peak guarantee.
Measured native-float reconstructed true peak reached +0.4 dBTP. A separate
host libopus check at 128 kbps with 20 ms frames found three excerpts below
full scale and one intense excerpt with a +0.7 dBTP reconstructed peak.
That decoded intense excerpt had 803 of 4,608,000 float samples above an
absolute value of 1.0 (0.0174 percent), with a peak of 1.06832. These are
codec reconstruction results, not out-of-range source samples. No lower
ceiling or adaptive gain controller was added to conceal them.

All eight audition FLACs are lossless encodings of their 48 kHz stereo PCM16
fixtures and decode byte-for-byte. They have no gain or ReplayGain tags.
The Opus check used host libopus, not Android MediaCodec. Neither these
measurements nor the builds establish actual earbud loudness on a street.

RECIPE, HISTORY AND METADATA

Composition snapshots retain engine=30 and record outputrev=1. Old saved
v30 recipes use the corrected output when loaded. Notes, instruments and
seed identity remain; waveforms and per-part amplitudes change as intended.
Previously rendered audio files are not rewritten by updating the app.

A related switching defect could carry the prior composition's backbeat
delay into a new song. Timing now comes from a separate seed-local random
stream using the same initial draws as reset. Both Next paths reproduce
their saved recipes; normal reset/load timing and composition randomness
are unchanged.

OGG and FLAC metadata expose ENGINE_VERSION=30, OUTPUT_MASTER_VERSION=1,
and the complete TECHNOMATIC_SONG_DATA recipe. Automatic Notes still contain
the seed and engine followed by the listener's optional notes; the technical
output revision does not clutter the editor. Missing or invalid revisions
in arbitrary legacy metadata input are omitted rather than invented.

BUILD AND INSTALLATION

The project keeps ndkVersion '30.0.14904198'. On the owner's machine, select
SDK root /home/tutan/Android/Sdk so Gradle uses the installed NDK at:
  /home/tutan/Android/Sdk/ndk/30.0.14904198

Open the source project in Android Studio using its included Gradle wrapper,
or run ./gradlew assembleDebug or ./gradlew assembleRelease. No SDK path
specific to the temporary build environment is included as local.properties.
AGENTS.md preserves the NDK and fixed-output requirements for future work.

The supplied debug APK is signed with a new temporary build certificate.
Its package is vip.thatiam.technomatic2105.debug. It cannot directly update
the previously supplied v30 debug APK, which has a different certificate.
Build the source using the same signing key as the existing installation
for an in-place update that retains app data and history.

The release APK uses vip.thatiam.technomatic2105 and is unsigned. Sign it
with the owner's release key before installing or distributing it. Using
the existing release key supports an in-place release update. Signing
keys and toolchain caches are not included in the source package.
